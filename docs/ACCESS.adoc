= People, guests and organisation access
:toc: left
:icons: font

Bookie decides which organisations each person can open. By default everyone in your Microsoft 365
sees every organisation you have connected. *Guests*, such as your external accountant, see nothing
until someone in your organisation gives them access.

== Members and guests

Bookie reads the person's Microsoft Entra `userType` when they sign in:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Type | Who and what they see

| Member | Someone in your own Microsoft 365. Sees every organisation while _Everyone in <org> can see
all organisations_ is on (the default); otherwise only the organisations ticked for them.
Members can always open Settings.
| Guest | Someone from another company invited to your Microsoft 365 (Entra B2B), such as your
accountant. Sees only the organisations ticked for them. No entry means no organisations. Guests
can't open Settings; they're asked to contact an administrator instead.
|===

Bookie looks the type up with Microsoft Graph (`GET /me?$select=userType,mail,userPrincipalName`)
using the sign-in token, and keeps it with the person's email in the encrypted session cookie.
Older Entra accounts can have a blank `userType`; Microsoft treats those as members, and so does
Bookie, unless the user principal name is a B2B guest's (`...#EXT#@...`). If Graph can't say (the
call fails, or the type is `Guest` or anything else), the person is treated as a guest. Sessions
from before this feature sign in again.

== Access entries

*Settings › People and access* (members only) lists one entry per person:

[source,json]
----
{
  "email": "accountant@example.com",
  "name": "Robin Example",
  "orgKeys": ["xero.{connectionId}.{xeroTenantId}"]
}
----

* `email` is matched case-insensitively with the address the person signs in with (their `mail`,
  or their home email for a guest).
* `orgKeys` lists the organisation keys they may open (the same keys as in portal URLs), or `"all"`
  for every organisation, including ones connected later.
* The list of organisations to tick includes Business Central companies hidden from the switcher.
  Hidden companies stay hidden for everyone until they're shown again.
* _Everyone in <org> can see all organisations_ (`membersSeeAll`, on by default) controls members.

Entries are part of the tenant's settings, sealed with the tenant's key and stored in the tenant's own
SharePoint like every other setting. No accounting data is involved.

Business Central still applies each person's own Business Central permissions: an entry can't give
anyone more than Business Central allows them. Xero connections are shared, so the entry is what
limits a guest's view of a Xero organisation.

== Where it's enforced

`listOrgs()` (`web/src/lib/connectors/registry.ts`) applies the entries for every request, and every
org key from a URL or request body is resolved against its result:

* the portal (`orgContext()`) and every server action that uses it;
* `getConnector()` (PDF downloads, attachments);
* Bookie's chat, tool and voice routes (`bookieContext()`);
* the Live feed's events route (`liveSource()`).

An organisation that doesn't exist and one the person can't open look the same: 404.

Every settings write (connections, Business Central, Live updates, people and access, the Xero
connect and callback routes) refuses guests, so a guest can't widen their own access.

== Signing in as a guest

A guest who signs in with the main *Sign in with Microsoft* button lands in their *home* tenant
(their own firm), not yours, because Bookie signs in through the multi-tenant `organizations`
authority. To sign in to your tenant they need a sign-in that uses your tenant's own authority.

Set `BOOKIE_GUEST_TENANTS` to the host tenants guests can sign in to:

----
BOOKIE_GUEST_TENANTS=<tenantId>:<Display Name>;<tenantId2>:<Name 2>
----

For each entry Bookie:

. registers an extra Microsoft Entra provider, `azure-ad-<first 8 characters of the tenant id>`,
  with that tenant as its authority and the same client id and secret;
. shows *Accountant or guest of <Name>? Sign in to <Name>* under the main button on the sign-in page.

Signing in through it gives a token for the host tenant, so Bookie opens the host's settings and
organisations, filtered by the guest's access entry.

Each provider needs its own redirect URI on the app registration:

----
https://<host>/api/auth/callback/azure-ad-<first 8 characters of the tenant id>
----

Add one per deployment address (for example the local dev server, the production site and any
custom domain).

== Giving your accountant access

. In Microsoft Entra, invite the accountant as a guest user.
. In Bookie, *Settings › People and access*, add them by email and tick the organisations they need.
. Send them to Bookie's sign-in page and ask them to use *Sign in to <your organisation>*.

== Testing

* Unit tests: `web/src/lib/auth/access.test.ts` (the rules) and
  `web/src/lib/connectors/registry-access.test.ts` (listOrgs, getConnector, Bookie, the Live feed and
  the settings actions, as a demo member and a demo guest).
* End to end: `web/tests/e2e/access.spec.ts` runs against a second dev server started with
  `BOOKIE_DEV_AUTH=demo-guest`: a fictional guest accountant whose seeded entry grants Contoso
  Coffee Roasters only. Like the other dev sign-ins, `demo-guest` is refused in production.
