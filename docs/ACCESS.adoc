= People, guests and organisation access
:toc: left
:icons: font

Bookie decides which organisations each person can open. By default everyone in your Microsoft 365
sees every organisation you have connected. *Guests*, such as your external accountant, see nothing
until someone in your organisation gives them access.

[[approved-organisations]]
== Approved organisations

Only organisations KnowAll AI has approved can use Bookie. When someone signs in, Bookie takes the
Microsoft Entra tenant id (the `tid` claim) from the verified sign-in and allows them only if it is:

* KnowAll AI's own tenant (`f36f6414-cb7d-4545-9cf2-7574f7b5c584`), or
* listed in the `APPROVED_TENANT_IDS` app setting: comma-separated tenant GUIDs, compared in lower
  case. Malformed entries are ignored with a warning.

A sign-in with a missing or malformed `tid` is never allowed; there is no default tenant.

Everyone else sees _Your organisation isn't set up on Bookie yet_, with their organisation's name
and tenant id, a *Request access* button (it opens their own mail app with a message to
`hello@knowall.ai` that already holds the app, organisation, tenant id and their email), the address
itself, and *Sign out*. They get no organisations, navigation or data: portal pages and server
actions send them to that page, and API routes answer 401 as if they were signed out. Bookie's public helper on the website
(`PUBLIC_BOOKIE_ENABLED`) doesn't need a sign-in and is unaffected.

Guests invited into an approved tenant (Entra B2B, such as an external accountant) sign in through
that tenant (see <<signing-in-as-a-guest>>), so their sign-in carries its tenant id and they are
allowed. If they use the main button instead they land in their own firm's tenant and see the
"not set up yet" page, which also offers the *Sign in to <Name>* links. Everything below (members,
guests, access entries) still applies on top.

=== Approving an organisation

. Ask the organisation for its tenant id: it is on the "not set up yet" page and in the access
  request email, or in the Entra admin centre under *Overview › Tenant ID*.
. Add it to the `APPROVED_TENANT_IDS` application setting on the `bookie-knowall` App Service
  (*Settings › Environment variables*), separated from the others by a comma, and save. The app
  restarts and the organisation can sign in straight away; nobody needs to sign in again.
. To remove an organisation, take its id out again. Its people are shown the "not set up yet" page
  on their next request.

If the organisation's guests sign in through a host tenant in `BOOKIE_GUEST_TENANTS`, that host
tenant must be approved as well.

Enforced in `getAuthContext()` and `requireAuth()` (`web/src/lib/auth/context.ts`), which every
page, server action and API route already uses, with the rule in
`web/src/lib/auth/approved-tenants.ts`.

== Members and guests

Bookie reads the person's Microsoft Entra `userType` when they sign in:

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Type | Who and what they see

| Member | Someone in your own Microsoft 365. Sees every organisation while _Everyone in <org> can see
all organisations_ is on (the default); otherwise only the organisations ticked for them.
Members can always open Settings.
| Guest | Someone from another company invited to your Microsoft 365 (Entra B2B), such as your
accountant. Sees only the organisations ticked for them. No entry means no organisations. Guests
can't open Settings; they're asked to contact an administrator instead.
|===

Bookie looks the type up with Microsoft Graph (`GET /me?$select=userType,mail,userPrincipalName`)
using the sign-in token, and keeps it with the person's email in the encrypted session cookie.
Older Entra accounts can have a blank `userType`; Microsoft treats those as members, and so does
Bookie, unless the user principal name is missing or is a B2B guest's (`...#EXT#@...`). If Graph can't say (the
call fails, or the type is `Guest` or anything else), the person is treated as a guest. Sessions
from before this feature sign in again.

== Access entries

*Settings › People and access* (members only) lists one entry per person:

[source,json]
----
{
  "userId": "{Entra object id}",
  "email": "accountant@example.com",
  "name": "Robin Example",
  "orgKeys": ["xero.{connectionId}.{xeroTenantId}"]
}
----

* `userId` is the person's Microsoft Entra object id, saved when they're picked from the directory
  (see <<people-picker>>). An entry with a `userId` matches only the account with that object id
  (the `oid` of the sign-in), so a mailbox address reused by another account never inherits
  someone's access.
* `email` is matched case-insensitively with the address the person signs in with (their `mail`,
  or their home email for a guest), for entries without a `userId`. Entries added before the
  picker have none and keep working; the portal marks them _Matched by email_. Picking the same
  person from the directory adds their `userId` and keeps their organisations.
* `orgKeys` lists the organisation keys they may open (the same keys as in portal URLs), or `"all"`
  for every organisation, including ones connected later.
* The list of organisations to tick includes Business Central companies hidden from the switcher.
  Hidden companies stay hidden for everyone until they're shown again.
* _Everyone in <org> can see all organisations_ (`membersSeeAll`, on by default) controls members.

Entries are part of the tenant's settings, sealed with the tenant's key and stored in the tenant's own
SharePoint like every other setting. No accounting data is involved.

[[people-picker]]
== Adding people from the directory

People are added with a people picker, not by typing an address. Only people in your Microsoft
Entra directory (members, and guests you've invited) can be added.

. Type at least two characters of a name or email. Bookie searches as you type (debounced), with
  the signed-in member's own delegated Graph token:
+
----
GET /v1.0/users?$search="displayName:<text>" OR "mail:<text>"
    &$select=id,displayName,mail,userPrincipalName,userType&$top=10
ConsistencyLevel: eventual
----
. Each result shows the name, the email (`mail`, or for a guest without one, the home address taken
  from their `...#EXT#@...` user principal name) and *Member* or *Guest*. A blank `userType` counts as
  a member unless the user principal name is a guest's, the same rule as at sign-in. The badge is a
  label only: what a person sees is decided by their sign-in.
. Choose someone with the mouse or the keyboard (arrow keys and Enter). Bookie looks them up again
  by object id on the server, then adds them with no organisations; tick the ones they need and
  *Save access*.

Searching and adding are members only, like every settings write, and validated on the server.

=== "Can't sign in yet"

Bookie's Entra app can require assignment (*Enterprise applications › Bookie › Properties ›
Assignment required*). Then only people assigned to Bookie, directly or through a group, can sign
in, whatever their entry says. For each listed person with a `userId`, the portal checks:

. `GET /servicePrincipals(appId='{AZURE_AD_CLIENT_ID}')?$select=id,appRoleAssignmentRequired`:
  Bookie's service principal in your tenant. If assignment isn't required, everyone can sign in.
. `GET /servicePrincipals/{id}/appRoleAssignedTo`: the users and groups assigned to Bookie.
. For someone not assigned directly, while groups are assigned:
  `POST /users/{id}/checkMemberGroups` with those groups.

If they aren't assigned, the entry says _Can't sign in yet: an administrator needs to assign them
to Bookie in Microsoft Entra_ with a link to Enterprise applications in the Entra admin centre. If
the check can't be made (a permission isn't granted, the signed-in member's directory role doesn't
allow it, or Graph fails), the portal shows nothing rather than guessing. Bookie never assigns
anyone: an administrator does that in Entra.

Microsoft Entra doesn't pass app assignment down nested groups, while `checkMemberGroups` is
transitive. Someone who is only in a nested group shows no hint, though they can't sign in; Bookie
errs on the side of saying nothing.

=== Permissions

All delegated, so Bookie acts as the signed-in member and sees only what they may see. They come
with the token Bookie already redeems for Microsoft Graph (`.default`), so they only need admin
consent on the app (see `docs/DEPLOYMENT.adoc`); the sign-in scopes don't change.

[cols="1,1,3"]
|===
| Permission | Needed for | Without it

| `User.ReadBasic.All` | The people picker | The picker says an administrator needs to approve
the permission. Existing entries keep working.
| `Application.Read.All` | The sign-in hint (service principal and its assignments) | No hint.
| `GroupMember.Read.All` | The sign-in hint for people assigned through a group | No hint for
people who aren't assigned directly, while groups are assigned.
|===

Reading assignments through the user (`GET /users/{id}/appRoleAssignments`) would need
`AppRoleAssignment.ReadWrite.All` (a write permission) or `Directory.Read.All`, so Bookie reads them
from its own service principal instead. Microsoft documents that reading the assignments needs the
signed-in member to hold a directory role such as Directory Readers; without one Graph refuses, and
the hint is left out.

`User.ReadBasic.All` reads the basic profile. If Graph leaves `userType` out of the answer, the
Member/Guest badge follows the user principal name as above.

Business Central still applies each person's own Business Central permissions: an entry can't give
anyone more than Business Central allows them. Xero connections are shared, so the entry is what
limits a guest's view of a Xero organisation.

== Where it's enforced

`listOrgs()` (`web/src/lib/connectors/registry.ts`) applies the entries for every request, and every
org key from a URL or request body is resolved against its result:

* the portal (`orgContext()`) and every server action that uses it;
* `getConnector()` (PDF downloads, attachments);
* Bookie's chat, tool and voice routes (`bookieContext()`);
* the Live feed's events route (`liveSource()`).

An organisation that doesn't exist and one the person can't open look the same: 404.

Every settings write (connections, Business Central, Live updates, people and access, the Xero
connect and callback routes) refuses guests, so a guest can't widen their own access.

[[signing-in-as-a-guest]]
== Signing in as a guest

A guest who signs in with the main *Sign in with Microsoft* button lands in their *home* tenant
(their own firm), not yours, because Bookie signs in through the multi-tenant `organizations`
authority. To sign in to your tenant they need a sign-in that uses your tenant's own authority.

Set `BOOKIE_GUEST_TENANTS` to the host tenants guests can sign in to:

----
BOOKIE_GUEST_TENANTS=<tenantId>:<Display Name>;<tenantId2>:<Name 2>
----

For each entry Bookie:

. registers an extra Microsoft Entra provider, `azure-ad-<first 8 characters of the tenant id>`,
  with that tenant as its authority and the same client id and secret;
. shows *Accountant or guest of <Name>? Sign in to <Name>* under the main button on the sign-in page.

Signing in through it gives a token for the host tenant, so Bookie opens the host's settings and
organisations, filtered by the guest's access entry.

Each provider needs its own redirect URI on the app registration:

----
https://<host>/api/auth/callback/azure-ad-<first 8 characters of the tenant id>
----

Add one per deployment address (for example the local dev server, the production site and any
custom domain).

== Giving your accountant access

. In Microsoft Entra, invite the accountant as a guest user. If Bookie requires assignment, assign
  them to Bookie in *Enterprise applications*.
. In Bookie, *Settings › People and access*, find them in the people picker and tick the
  organisations they need.
. Send them to Bookie's sign-in page and ask them to use *Sign in to <your organisation>*.
